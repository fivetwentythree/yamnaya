Wong et al. (2017) Genetic history and admixture involving Yamnaya and northern Eurasian populations are relevant to the overall aDNA review. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract reports Yamnaya-related admixture and ancient gene flow relevant to population movement; it makes no substantive Indo-European claim.
Chintalapati et al. (2022) Ancient-genomic dating reconstructs Steppe pastoralist ancestry and migration chronology. INCLUDE - MIGRATION + LANGUAGE Abstract addresses the timing and spread of Steppe ancestry across Europe and explicitly relates the findings to Indo-European language spread.
Wang et al. (2021) Genome-wide data address Eurasian population formation and Yamnaya-related ancestry in eastern Eurasia. INCLUDE - MIGRATION + LANGUAGE Abstract reports Yamnaya ancestry reaching western Mongolia after about 3000 BCE and explicitly proposes compatibility with proto-Tocharian dispersal.
Librado et al. (2021) Ancient horse genomics is relevant to the group’s technology and mobility questions and directly evaluates a proposed Yamnaya expansion mechanism. BACKGROUND ONLY The abstract rejects a simple link between horseback riding, Yamnaya expansion and Indo-European spread, but it does not provide human genetic evidence of Yamnaya migration.
Scorrano et al. (2021) The study assesses the genetic and sex-biased effects of Steppe migration on Bronze Age European populations. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract directly addresses migration scale, geographic impact and male-biased Steppe ancestry in Corded Ware and Bell Beaker populations.
Linderholm et al. (2020) Genomic, kinship and isotope evidence informs Corded Ware population history and mobility. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract reports Steppe paternal ancestry, local maternal lineages and local/non-local kinship in a Corded Ware community connected to Yamnaya.
Lazaridis et al. (2025) - duplicate Exact duplicate of canonical collection item KZVYLCU9; removed before title/abstract screening. EXCLUDE Duplicate record; the canonical copy is screened separately.
Furholt (2018) The article critiques how aDNA migration narratives are interpreted in third-millennium European archaeology. BACKGROUND ONLY Abstract provides methodological and conceptual guidance for interpreting mobility and migration but does not present a principal new migration dataset.
Tassi et al. (2017) Ancient DNA tests demographic relationships between Globular Amphorae groups and Pontic-steppe-related populations. INCLUDE - MIGRATION + LANGUAGE Abstract compares Steppe and Anatolian language-spread models and distinguishes cultural influence from movement of people.
Ringbauer et al. (2024) The method and application reveal close genealogical connections among ancient Eurasian populations. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract reports substantial recent co-ancestry between Yamnaya and Corded Ware groups, directly informing their demographic relationship.
Dzhaubermezov et al. (2026) Systematic review and meta-analysis addresses Caucasus demographic history, Steppe gene flow and Yamnaya expansion. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract synthesizes Yamnaya-linked Steppe gene flow in a major Eurasian corridor; language-genetics is mentioned mainly as broader context.
Lazaridis et al. (2025) Ancient DNA reconstructs Yamnaya formation, expansion and ancestry relationships. INCLUDE - MIGRATION + LANGUAGE Abstract details migration and admixture routes preceding Yamnaya formation, the 3300 BCE expansion, and an explicit proto-Indo-Anatolian interpretation.
Nikitin et al. (2025) Genome-wide data reconstruct multiple migration waves and Yamna formation in the North Pontic region. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract directly identifies three migration waves, admixture processes and Yamna expansion beginning around 3300 BCE.
Papac et al. (2021) A regional archaeogenetic time transect documents migration, Steppe ancestry and population turnover. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract addresses Corded Ware and Bell Beaker demographic change, steppe ancestry, assimilation and replacement during and around the target period.
Mathieson et al. (2018) Genome-wide data address Steppe contact, ancestry formation and population replacement in southeastern Europe. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract provides earlier evidence for Steppe contact and ancestry formation that contextualizes later Steppe migrations and replacement.
Olalde et al. (2018) Genome-wide data distinguish cultural diffusion from migration and document Steppe-related ancestry and replacement. INCLUDE - MIGRATION Abstract traces the continued east-to-west spread of Steppe-related ancestry through Bell Beaker-associated migration and major population replacement.
Allentoft et al. (2015) Ancient genomes across Eurasia document large-scale Bronze Age migrations and replacements. INCLUDE - MIGRATION + LANGUAGE Abstract addresses Eurasian migration and states that the findings are consistent with hypothesized Early Bronze Age Indo-European dispersal.
Haak et al. (2015) Genome-wide data document large migration into Europe from the Eurasian Steppe. INCLUDE - MIGRATION + LANGUAGE Abstract directly links Steppe migration evidence to support for a Steppe-origin hypothesis for some Indo-European languages.
